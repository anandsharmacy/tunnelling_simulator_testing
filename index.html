About Quantum Tunneling

Quantum tunneling is a quantum mechanical phenomenon where a particle passes through a potential energy barrier that classical physics would deem impenetrable. It arises because particles exhibit wave-like behavior governed by the Schrödinger equation — the wavefunction ψ has a non-zero amplitude on both sides of the barrier.

The simulation displays scattering components: incident, reflected, and transmitted waves. The barrier region shows exponential decay for tunneling (E < V₀), along with the probability density |ψ|² overlay when enabled.

Use the Time Dependent / Independent menu for temporal behavior and per-wave visibility toggles, while keeping the same physical controls for barrier height, width, and particle energy.

The simulator also renders the potential V(x) in steel-blue.

Real-World Applications

Nuclear fusion in stars · Scanning Tunneling Microscopes (STM) · Tunnel diodes · Flash memory (NAND) · Enzyme catalysis · Alpha decay

About this Simulator

The TDSE is solved with the Crank–Nicolson implicit scheme — unconditionally stable, unitary (norm-preserving), and 2nd-order accurate in both Δx and Δt.

Press ▶ Simulator on the right to view the live wavepacket animation.

Equations Used

Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation

Governing equationiℏ ∂ψ/∂t = Ĥψ = [−ℏ²/2m · ∂²/∂x² + V(x)] ψ

Crank–Nicolson Scheme

CN update (α = Δt/2ℏ)(I + iα·Ĥ) ψⁿ⁺¹ = (I − iα·Ĥ) ψⁿ

Initial Gaussian Wavepacket

ψ(x,0)ψ = (2πσ²)^(−1/4) · exp[−(x−x₀)²/4σ²] · e^(ik₀x)

Transmission & Reflection

ProbabilitiesT = ∫_{x>x_R} |ψ|² dx  |  R = ∫_{x<x_L} |ψ₋|² dx  (x_L, x_R = barrier edges; ψ₋ = left-moving part; plus what the edges absorbed)

WKB Estimate (rectangular barrier)

Tunneling probabilityT ≈ exp[−2L √(2m(V₀−E)) / ℏ]

How the Simulator Works

  1. Grid init: N = 1399 uniform points over x ∈ [−160, 160] in units with ℏ = m = 1 (shown for an electron: 1 unit = 0.276 nm, energies in eV).
  2. Potential V(x): Chosen barrier profile, averaged over each grid cell so its width is exact.
  3. Wavepacket: Normalised Gaussian ψ(x,0) with momentum k₀ and spread σ, placed at x₀ = −120 (−33 nm). With the continuous source on, a fresh copy is added there at regular intervals.
  4. Hamiltonian: Kinetic energy via central differences → tridiagonal matrix H with V(x) on diagonal.
  5. CN matrices: A = I + iαH, B = I − iαH assembled; Thomas forward-sweep precomputed once.
  6. Time loop: (1) RHS = B·ψⁿ, (2) solve A·ψⁿ⁺¹ = RHS via Thomas O(N), (3) absorb outgoing waves in damping bands near ±160. No renormalisation: the norm only falls as probability leaves through the edges.
  7. Rendering: Scattering components (incident, reflected, transmitted) and V(x) steel-blue, with optional |ψ|² overlay.
  8. Readouts: T = probability past the barrier, R = left-moving probability before it (each plus what the edges absorbed), so the incoming packet never counts as reflected. With the continuous source on, they show the steady-state split.
